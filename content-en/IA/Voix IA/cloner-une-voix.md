# Cloning a voice: technique and ethical/legal stakes

The [previous chapter](/?c=ia&s=voix-ia&p=modeles-modernes-synthese) mentions that some models can imitate a voice from a short sample. This chapter develops that technique, **voice cloning**, and above all its stakes: it's the most sensitive notion in this section, with no equivalent as direct on the text or image side.

## How a model captures "a voice"

A cloning model extracts, from a reference audio sample, a **speaker embedding**: a [vector](/?c=mathematiques&p=vecteurs-et-produit-scalaire) of numbers that summarizes that voice's characteristics (timbre, average pitch, accent), separately from the content of what's said:

```text
Reference sample (a few seconds) -> extraction -> speaker embedding (a vector)
                                                                      │
Text to read -> [synthesis model, conditioned on this embedding] -> audio in this voice
```

The same principle as [word embeddings](/?c=ia&s=nlp-llm&p=nlp-llm): a voice close to another (similar general timbre) has a close embedding, two very different voices have distant embeddings.

| Amount of reference audio | Typical result |
|---|---|
| A few seconds (*zero-shot*) | General resemblance, sometimes a few artifacts on sounds rare in the sample |
| A few minutes | Noticeably better, more stable resemblance |
| Several hours (dedicated fine-tuning, see next chapter) | Quality closest to the original voice |

## Ethical and legal stakes: consent and audio deepfakes

Cloning a voice without the consent of the person concerned poses a direct problem, regardless of the technical quality of the result:

> **Pitfall:** treating voice cloning as a mere technical feat, without considering whether the person whose voice is cloned consented to it. Audio generated in someone's voice can be used for fraud (impersonation during a phone call, a technique already exploited to deceive employees or relatives), disinformation (making a public figure "say" things they never said), or reputational harm without any classic copyright law clearly applying.
>
> **Best practice:** obtain explicit, documented consent before cloning an identifiable person's voice, and design the final product to remain traceable back to its source (see watermarking below), not just rely on the absence of a complaint.

A person's voice is itself **biometric data**: [EU AI regulation](/?c=ia&s=production-et-gouvernance&p=reglementation-europeenne-ia) imposes specific transparency obligations on AI-generated or -manipulated audio content (disclosing that content is artificial); this chapter doesn't go into the legal detail already covered by that dedicated chapter.

## Watermarking generated content

One technical response to the disinformation risk is to embed, in the generated audio itself, an inaudible mark that allows identifying it afterward as AI-produced:

```text
Audio generated by a cloning model
      │
      ▼
Watermarking: a signal inaudible to the human ear, encoded into the audio
      │
      ▼
A dedicated detector can recover this mark and confirm: "this audio is AI-generated"
```

> **Pitfall:** treating watermarking as an absolute guarantee. A watermark can be removed or degraded by compression or later audio processing, deliberate or not; it's only a protection against a use that doesn't actively try to circumvent it.
>
> **Best practice:** treat watermarking as an additional layer of traceability, not an infallible guarantee, to combine with documented consent and clear usage policies on the provider's side.

## Key takeaways

|---|---|
| **Key takeaways** | Voice cloning extracts a speaker embedding from a reference sample, which then conditions a synthesis model. Quality improves with the amount of reference audio. The consent of the cloned person is the central issue, distinct from the technical feat; EU AI regulation imposes transparency obligations on this type of content. Inaudible watermarking helps with traceability, without an absolute guarantee. |
| **Tools you can use** | A speaker embedding to capture a voice from a reference sample. Inaudible watermarking for traceability of generated content. |
| **Pitfalls to avoid** | Cloning a voice without documented consent. Treating watermarking as an absolute guarantee against misuse. |
| **Best practices** | Obtain and document explicit consent before cloning any identifiable voice. Combine watermarking, documented consent, and clear usage policies. |
